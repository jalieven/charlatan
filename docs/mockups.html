Charlatan · Design review

Charlatan Mockups

Eighteen frames covering all nine screens, S1–S9, in flow order — including the reveal's four states, the second-tie warning, and the three-act result. In-app copy is Dutch — the default locale per §6.4. One running scenario throughout: six players, koffie/thee, Tom is the Charlatan. Annotations under each frame say what to validate.

S1 · Setup

One screen, five decisions

Names in seating order (the pass order), the four game options, and Start. Type does the hierarchy: player names in weight, options as quiet rows, one loud button. The accent color is part of the design, not a setting.

Launch — resume or new game

An unfinished round survives indefinitely (§4). Choosing "nieuw spel" is the explicit end of the old round; resuming re-enters via the handoff interstitial, never on private data (§6.3).

S2 · Handoff

The privacy gate

Every transition between two players' private screens passes through this interstitial — reveal and vote alike (§6.3). Nothing secret is on screen; the slide is a deliberate gesture so nobody inherits the previous player's screen.

S3 · Reveal

One thumb, everything snaps back

The grammar of §3.3: covered resting state → swipe-up-hold for the word → long-press for the role card → optional slide-to-Whisper → slide to pass. Nothing secret survives a lifted finger. The civilian role card (not shown) is geometrically identical to the Charlatan's.

3 · Word — Whispered

A Whispered player sees two words in identical styling and random order — their assigned word and the distractor. Position and treatment never betray which one was Whispered (§3.6). Eva now mistrusts both. Monochrome on purpose — the accent is reserved for the verdict.

4 · Role card — held, Charlatan

The card grows upward from the held role button, so the dashed Whisper target sits directly above the thumb: hold → one thumb-width slide up → release-to-commit (§3.3). Releasing anywhere else closes the card. The civilian card shows a same-position target that just closes; the target is absent once the round's Whisper is burned — the accepted tell (§3.6).

S4 · Clue entry & Ledger

Type it, then say it aloud

The active player types their one-word clue, confirms, and says it aloud (§3.5). The Ledger — every clue this round, grouped by cycle — is always visible below. Warnings are private and never block. No timer anywhere.

2 · Extra cycle after the second tie

The tie-stakes banner is a hard requirement: it appears the moment the second consecutive tie happens and persists through this cycle and the next ballot (§3.7). Bram, voted out earlier, is struck from the order — which stays otherwise stable within the round (§3.4). Cycle counters grow past the base 2 with every tie/ejection loop.

S5 · S6 · S7 — Vote, verdict & the steal

Private ballots, open verdicts

Ballots are individual and private via the handoff (§3.7): no self-votes, no abstentions, eliminated players excluded. The verdict announces roles openly; a caught Charlatan immediately gets one typed guess at the real word (§3.8).

S6 · Verdict — second consecutive tie

The mandatory stakes visualization (§3.7): the inverted block is the loudest thing monochrome can say. It stays on screen through the following clue cycle and ballot. A third tie ends the round for the Charlatans.

S7 · The steal

Typed, one attempt, case-insensitive with singular/plural tolerance (§3.8). The guess is shown at the result so the table can judge a near-miss. A correct guess wins the round for all Charlatans.

S8 · Result

Three acts, narrated by the phone-holder

One act per screen, advanced by tap, three-dot strip, back-swipe allowed — everything here is public. The words and the replay share a single drill-in behind one button on the verdict (§4, step 8); the drill-in carries no dots — it is not an act. Scenario: round 4 — Bram (a burger) was voted out mid-round, then Tom was caught on the fourth vote; steal missed, burgers win. Eliminated Bram still scores the team win (§3.9), but had no vote in the ejecting ballot.
